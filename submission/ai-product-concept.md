# Proposal: TEA Climate Design Mentor

TEA Climate Design Mentor would help architecture students and early-career built-environment professionals reason about climate-responsive design. Its purpose is to turn scattered guidance into a structured learning conversation, not to approve buildings.

A learner supplies a location, design stage, orientation and a brief describing the site. The mentor asks for missing context, explains passive-design principles, compares alternatives and produces a checklist with citations. It invites the learner to justify choices about shading, ventilation, materials and water rather than merely accepting generated answers.

The first version would use a hosted language-model API with retrieval over a small, licensed collection of TEA learning materials and approved climate-design references. Retrieval provides inspectable evidence; structured outputs keep recommendations, assumptions and uncertainties separate. Documents would carry source dates and geographic applicability. When evidence is missing, the system would say so.

Build a limited prototype with an upload-free questionnaire, curated document index, source-linked answers and educator feedback. Evaluate it using real learning scenarios and a rubric for citation accuracy, regional relevance and explanation quality. Compare several models against that rubric before choosing one. Start with one climate region and expand only after expert review.

Risks include hallucinated guidance, stale regulations, regional bias, copyright misuse and sensitive site data. Collect minimal information, provide deletion controls and prohibit confidential uploads in the pilot. An architect or educator must review technical outputs before practical application. The mentor cannot replace building codes, engineering calculations, professional judgement or local consultation. Cost limits, logging without personal information and periodic source audits would support responsible operation.
